1/ Most businesses calculate their software spend by adding up the monthly invoices. That number is wrong by 30 to 50 percent. Here's where the rest of the money goes ↓

2/ A 10-person team paying $9,600/year in SaaS licensing is not paying $9,600/year for software. They're also paying to connect tools that don't share data natively. That layer costs $4,000 to $18,000/year for a 15-person org connecting 5 tools.

3/ Per-seat pricing means every new hire increases your software bill across every tool simultaneously. A team growing from 10 to 25 people sees licensing costs 2.5x before adding a single new tool.

4/ Most teams activate 30 to 50 percent of the features in their current plan tier. The other half is embedded in the subscription fee and can't be removed without downgrading. You're paying for the possibility of using features, not the actuality.

5/ The migration cost that keeps businesses subscribed too long: $2,000 to $4,500 in internal labor for a CRM migration. The ongoing cost of staying with a tool that meets 65% of needs: roughly $6,300/year in workaround labor. After one year, the migration pays for itself.
