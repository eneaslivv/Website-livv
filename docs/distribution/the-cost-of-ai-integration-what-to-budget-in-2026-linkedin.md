Most early AI budgets are wrong by a factor of five to twenty. The API rate card is easy to find. The development cost is not visible until a quote arrives.

We work on AI integrations for mid-sized businesses. The pattern in early planning conversations is consistent: founders and product teams focus on per-token API pricing because it is published. They arrive with estimates based on usage volume times token cost. Those estimates miss the three other budget layers that account for the majority of what an AI integration actually costs.

A complete AI integration budget has four layers. The model API is the usage-based charge for sending inputs and receiving outputs. SaaS tooling covers subscriptions for products that sit between the API and the application. Development work is the engineering cost of building the integration and connecting it to existing systems. Ongoing operations covers hosting, maintenance, and model version updates across the product's life.

Development work is typically 80 to 90 percent of first-year spend. That number does not appear until a vendor submits a quote. An AI feature addition to an existing product costs $8,000 to $25,000. A customer-facing chatbot with retrieval over company content costs $20,000 to $60,000. A document-processing system costs $25,000 to $80,000. An AI agent workflow costs $60,000 to $150,000.

What the numbers look like across a full first year:

- API costs run $20 to $500 per month for most integrations at realistic business volumes
- A simple AI feature addition totals $14,000 to $49,000 in year one, including operations
- A customer-facing chatbot with RAG totals $32,000 to $108,000 in year one
- AI document processing totals $43,000 to $152,000 in year one
- AI agent workflows total $84,000 to $270,000 in year one

The range on each figure is wide because scope varies. Once data complexity and feature count are specified, the estimates tighten considerably.
