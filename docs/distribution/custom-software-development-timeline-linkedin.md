16 to 28 weeks from discovery to production. That's the real range for a focused custom software build. Most of the variation doesn't come from development speed.

After tracking dozens of custom software projects, the phase breakdown is consistent. Discovery takes 2 to 4 weeks. Design takes 3 to 8 weeks and overlaps with the end of discovery. Development runs 8 to 16 weeks for a focused MVP. QA and user acceptance testing add 2 to 4 weeks on top of that. Post-launch stabilization should be planned and budgeted separately, for 30 to 60 days after go-live.

Development is the longest phase, but it's also the most predictable once requirements and designs are locked before the build starts. The unpredictable part is everything that happens first. A scope addition after the requirements document is finalized costs 1.5x to 2x what the same feature would have cost during discovery, because the disruption to the build sequence is priced in addition to the feature itself.

The three factors that most reliably push timelines past the original estimate:

- Mid-project scope additions after requirements are finalized
- Slow stakeholder availability during design review (a 3-day review round becomes 10 when the approver is managing competing priorities)
- Third-party integrations with inconsistent API documentation: a Stripe integration takes 2 to 4 days; a legacy ERP with undocumented edge cases can take 2 to 3 weeks

Most software ships 5 to 15 bugs in the first week of real usage that didn't appear in QA or user acceptance testing. Those aren't signs of poor testing. They're what happens when real users interact with the system in ways the test suite didn't model. Budget for them explicitly, as a separate line item from the build estimate.
