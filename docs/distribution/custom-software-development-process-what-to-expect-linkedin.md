Most custom software project overruns trace to a single conversation: the one where a founder asked for a price before discovery was complete.

The number they got was real. It was built on assumptions, not on knowledge. Six months later, the project cost twice the estimate and took three times as long. That outcome is predictable. Each phase of a custom build produces decisions. When a phase gets compressed, those decisions get made later at higher cost, or they become operational problems after launch.

Custom software development follows five phases: discovery, design, build, ship, maintain. The cost of a decision is inversely proportional to how early it is made. A requirement settled in discovery costs little. The same requirement settled during build, after code was written around a different assumption, costs two to four times more to address.

Scope changes after design is complete are the most common cause of overruns. Integration complexity discovered during build is the second. Client review delays that break the incremental feedback loop are the third. A discovery phase that surfaces decisions early reduces all of these.

Five things worth knowing before the first quote arrives:

- Discovery ($5,000-$15,000) is the most frequently compressed phase. A well-run discovery produces a scope document both parties can read independently and describe consistently.
- Design ($8,000-$25,000) translates those decisions into specs. A design phase that ends with open questions is a design phase that did not finish.
- Build ($20,000-$150,000+) is iterative, not a single handoff. Client reviews at each checkpoint are the mechanism that catches gaps before they compound.
- Data migration during ship commonly adds $2,000-$10,000. Historical data is almost always messier than the scope assumed.
- Maintenance ($1,000-$4,000/month) is a real line item. A system with no maintenance accumulates security exposure over a 2-3 year window.

Total for a small-business application: $40,000 to $120,000. Timeline: 14 to 21 weeks for a focused workflow app.
