The published Salesforce price is $165/user/month. The actual 3-year cost of running it is never published.

Most comparisons stop at the license number. What moves the real cost is the admin line and the customization bills that accumulate after go-live.

A 10-person team on Salesforce Enterprise pays $19,800 per year in license fees. Year one, once implementation ($20,000 to $45,000), data migration, and training are included, comes to $44,800 to $82,800. Years two and three add a fractional administrator at $30,000 to $60,000 annually, plus $8,000 to $25,000 per year in active customization. The 3-year total for a 10-person team: $160,000 to $310,000. The admin cost alone accounts for $60,000 to $180,000 of that range.

A custom CRM built by a boutique studio costs $45,000 to $120,000. Add hosting ($200 to $800/month) and a maintenance retainer ($1,500 to $4,000/month). In a worked example for a 12-person B2B team with two product lines and a sales-to-CS handoff workflow, the custom path totaled $163,100 over 3 years. Salesforce for the same team: $234,240. The custom CRM was $71,000 cheaper and required no ongoing reconfiguration for the two-product-line workflow that Salesforce handled through custom objects and automation rules.

Salesforce is still the right call when the workflow is standard, the team is above 40 seats, or existing platform certifications matter for compliance. The case for custom gets stronger when year-one Salesforce customization exceeds $30,000.

Key numbers from the comparison:

- Salesforce Enterprise: $165/user/month; Professional lacks API access, pushing most mid-market teams to the more expensive tier
- Year one for a 10-person Enterprise team: $44,800 to $82,800
- Fractional Salesforce admin: $30,000 to $60,000 per year, rarely included in early estimates
- CPQ implementation: $30,000 to $120,000 on top of the base license
- 12-person, two-product-line scenario: Salesforce $234,240 vs custom CRM $163,100 over 3 years

When the workflow doesn't fit the standard model, neither does the cost. The full year-by-year model is on the LIVV blog.
