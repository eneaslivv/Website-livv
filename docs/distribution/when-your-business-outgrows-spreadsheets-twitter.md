1/ Most spreadsheets were built by one person and are now used by five. When two people edit different copies, by Friday three versions exist and none is authoritative. ↓

2/ There are four specific failure patterns:
- Version conflicts between team members
- Formula logic only one person understands
- Reconciliation work that appears on nobody's job description
- No way to run a report without a manual audit

3/ The failure threshold is predictable. A spreadsheet edited by one person is stable. A spreadsheet edited simultaneously by two or more people in an operational context will consistently produce inconsistent data.

4/ Two paths to replace it:

SaaS: $15-$100/user/month. A five-person team pays $75-$1,500/month.

Custom software: $15,000-$60,000 for a focused internal tool. Delivery in 8-16 weeks.

5/ The cost of staying on the spreadsheet is rarely counted. At $35-$75/hour for the person managing the file, and 2-8 hours per week, the annual opportunity cost runs $3,640 to $31,200.

6/ Migration takes 4-12 weeks. The parallel-running period (both systems used simultaneously to catch discrepancies) takes 2-4 weeks and should not be skipped.
