Most small businesses exploring AI automation spend longer evaluating tools than it takes to run a first project. The bottleneck is almost never the technology.

It's identifying the right workflow to start with.

After watching dozens of AI automation projects at small businesses land well or go sideways over the past two years, the pattern holds consistently. The companies that get a working first implementation quickly all start by listing their workflows, not by selecting AI tools. The companies that stall longest start by asking which AI to use.

Here's the actual cost structure in 2026, since that's usually where the conversation gets stuck first:

**Tier 1 -- configure an existing AI tool** (AI email, meeting transcription, document parsers): $50-$500/month, live in 1-4 weeks, no development required.

**Tier 2 -- custom AI integration** (API-connected, prompt-engineered for your specific workflow): $5,000-$25,000 to build, $100-$1,000/month ongoing, 4-12 weeks to production.

**Tier 3 -- custom AI agent** (multi-step, tool-calling, variable-input workflows): $20,000-$80,000 to build, higher inference costs, 8-20 weeks.

Most small businesses in 2026 have a viable first project in tier one or tier two.

The most common failure mode is not a technology problem. It's automating a workflow that wasn't clearly defined before the project started. If your team handles a task inconsistently today, the AI will be inconsistent in the same ways, but the inconsistency arrives at higher volume and is harder to catch.

The most extractable findings from the full piece:

- Processing 500 documents/month costs approximately $1-$5 in model API costs -- the raw inference is not the expensive part
- Ongoing maintenance ($500-$2,000/month) is the most consistently underestimated line item over a 2-3 year horizon
- A good first project runs frequently enough to generate evaluation data within weeks, not months
- A task that occurs twice a month takes six months to produce enough examples to evaluate
- Errors should be catchable before they cause downstream harm -- miscategorized invoice that triggers payment vs. miscategorized ticket caught in review queue

A good first project has narrow, well-defined scope; output that can be verified correct or wrong; sufficient frequency; and low cost-per-error during calibration. Those four criteria narrow most workflow inventories from four or five candidates down to one.
