Most AI development proposals arrive before the partner has asked about your data.

That's the first signal on fit. Not the portfolio. Not the pricing model. Whether they asked about your actual workflows and data before recommending anything.

We've seen enough AI development proposals to know what separates firms that can scope work accurately from those that can't. The selection decision matters more than the model choice. Most AI projects fail because the scope was wrong, the integration was underestimated, or the team left without enough context to keep the system running. All three trace back to the partner selection, not the API.

There are four engagement models in 2026, and they price very differently. A boutique AI studio (4-20 people) runs $25,000-$120,000 per project, with a paid discovery phase of $3,500-$8,000 before the full contract. A large full-service agency (50+ people) runs $120,000-$350,000 for a six-month project, plus $4,000-$10,000/month in support. Senior freelance AI specialists charge $120-$220/hour. Embedded technical leads charge $1,200-$2,400/day for a 3-6 month engagement.

Most AI projects that go wrong chose the wrong engagement model, not the wrong vendor within one.

The red flags that appear before you sign:

- Partner proposes fine-tuning before anyone has reviewed your data
- Scope document describes the architecture rather than what the system will do
- No production references exist, or all case studies are "currently in progress"
- The proposal doesn't mention how the system will fail or how failures will be handled
- Retainer pricing is proposed at the start of an initial build

A paid discovery phase ($3,500-$8,000) should produce a scope document, a data readiness assessment, a technical architecture recommendation, and a fixed-price or milestone-based estimate. If the estimate doesn't fit, you exit having spent a fraction of the full project cost to find that out.

The most useful question to ask any AI development partner: what was the total cost of the last comparable project they completed, including all change orders and the first 90 days of post-delivery support? The gap between initial estimate and final cost tells you more than any reference call will.
